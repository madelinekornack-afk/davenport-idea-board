Audit your property manager

Owners upload the monthly property-manager statement. The app checks every charge against the management contract, tracks all-time spend per property, and builds the evidence pack for a dispute. The general form, in Steve's words, is auditing any variable monthly vendor bill: "confidently and effortlessly dispute unfair charges."

Steve, Nov 3: "The more abstract idea there is automatically auditing contract/agreement terms when you're paying some vendor for a service each month, especially when that cost can vary month to month. I'm sure some version of this problem exists in many industries but I've just been seeing it recently in the property management world."

How it fits together

Everything it does

Built, in useReads any statementOwners forward the monthly PM statement by email or upload the PDF. Every line item is extracted, whatever software the PM uses.
Built, in useKnows the contractThe management agreement is read once: management fee, maintenance markup, leasing fee, approval threshold, pass-through rules.
Built, in useChecks every chargeEach line is tested against the contract. A 10% markup billed at 15%, a "coordination fee" that does not exist in the agreement, an unapproved repair above the limit.
Built, in useCatches paying twiceThe same repair on the same unit a few months apart, or a vendor invoice that was already reimbursed. The landlord forums call this "paying for the same problem twice."
Built, in usePer-property spend, all timeEvery dollar by property, category and vendor across every statement, so the owner finally has a number to compare against.
Built, in useBuilds the dispute packThe charge, the clause it violates, the prior statements that show the pattern, and a letter. Fireworks' read: the recovery workflow is the product, the audit is the hook.
Built, in useGrades the managerA report card per PM, so an owner with three managers knows which one to drop.
ProposedAny variable vendor billSteve, Nov 3: "automatically auditing contract terms when you're paying some vendor for a service each month, especially when that cost can vary." HOA, lawn, cleaning, SaaS.
ProposedMaintenance history that follows the ownerWhat landlords actually asked for on BiggerPockets: the record of what was fixed, by whom, for how much, that survives switching PMs. Orca already holds the verified version.
ProposedPM-branded trust portalFlip the channel: the PM offers the audit to its owners as proof of honesty. Avoids the conflict of selling against Orca's own customers.

Where the group is on it

Why it is rank 2It sits on Orca, which Phil calls one of the two real edges: Steve sees the sloppy invoicing daily, and Orca's verified maintenance data is the moat nobody else has. Neither Phil nor Steve has a packaging doubt about it the way they do about Clark.
Why market is a 3, not a 4The 5M owner-to-PM relationships in the Fireworks analysis is funnel size, not buyers. Only owners who use a PM and suspect them are in play, and most of those switch PMs rather than buy software. Havyn, cited at $19 to 99/mo, could not be found. DoorVault gives the audit away free.
Channel conflictAn owner-side auditor is aimed at Orca's own PM customers. The PM-branded trust portal is the way around it, and it is also what the landlord forums describe wanting.
What landlords asked forAcross three BiggerPockets threads, nobody asked to audit a statement. They asked for every tenant and vendor message in one thread, maintenance tracked from report to payment, a maintenance history that does not vanish, and hidden or creeping fees. The audit is the hook; the record is the product.
Phil's one-month ruleThis is a self-serve web product for owners, so a beta is a one-month build. Selling it through PMs instead turns it into a sales cycle, which breaks the rule.
Where it standsSteve is considering an MVP as an owner-facing surface on Orca. Design concept exists, not iterated. Whiteboard question for tonight: owner-direct, or PM-branded?

What each piece earns today

Appfigures estimates, US, iPhone, last 30 days (Oct 12 to Nov 10, 2026). The short version: owner-side landlord software does not monetize on the App Store at all, so these charts mostly show downloads, and the main finding is the absence.

Nobody sells this on the App StoreEvery real landlord tool here is free on iPhone and bills on the web: TurboTenant, RentRedi, Baselane, Innago, Stessa. The App Store cannot size this market, which is itself the finding. The buyer is a web user with a few properties, not a consumer browsing an app store.
The audit itself has no appDoorVault and Kestrel, the two products that do the statement audit, are web-only. The keyword "doorvault" returns agent and PM tools. There is no incumbent on the phone to displace, and no proof on the phone that owners pay.
The money is on the PM sideBuildium, AppFolio, DoorLoop and Yardi sell to property managers, and their resident portals do 50K to 135K downloads a month. That is the side Orca already sells to, which argues for the PM-branded portal over the owner-direct app.
The App Store's "landlord" is a gameRent Please Landlord Sim makes $158K a month. Real landlord apps combined make about $2K. Consumer intent for "landlord" on the phone is entertainment.
