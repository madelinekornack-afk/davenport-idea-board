The legacy at-home test companies, same measure

Everlywell and LetsGetChecked, the 2015-era kit companies, on the phone.

EverlywellLetsGetCheckedInsideTracker

Everlywell's October value is an Appfigures recount and is dropped; its run rate is about 100 ratings a month, flat for two years. LetsGetChecked is under 20 and falling. InsideTracker is dead on mobile.

Rythm is the one with momentumIts app went live on October 7 and the model already puts it at 113K US installs in 30 days, more than Function has done in any month. The ratings have not caught up (300 total), so part of that is a launch campaign. Either way it is the newest and fastest-moving company of its kind, which is what a distribution partner wants.
Function has peaked on the phoneIt is the scaled player (25K ratings, $499 a year, reported $2.5B valuation in 2025) and its monthly new ratings are down six-fold from January. A company that size does not need Maxinomics; it needs a growth story, which is a different conversation.
Superpower is the early oneLaunched the app in January, 1.8K ratings, growth stalled in the summer. It is the company most likely to trade equity for an audience that converts.
The 2015 cohort never made the phone workEverlywell, LetsGetChecked and InsideTracker have nothing happening in the App Store. Apple Ads on "at home blood test": nobody is buying. The category is being re-founded, not fought over.

What this says for idea 8

Three first conversations1. Rythm: the product on the card, one month in, with the install curve of a company that is spending to grow and would rather not. 2. Superpower: early and stalled, the classic audience-for-equity fit. 3. Function: only if the ask is a content partnership, not equity.
What to verify before any of themWhether Rythm's 113K is organic or bought (ask for their install source split), what each pays per acquired customer today, and whether a Maxinomics video converts at a tenth of that. One sponsored video with a tracking link answers all three for under $10K of opportunity cost.
What the data cannot sayRevenue. All of these bill on the web, so the App Store shows installs and nothing else. Function has disclosed numbers publicly; the others have not.
ScopeUS, iPhone, App Store only. Android, web sign-ups and web billing are not here, and most of these companies sell on the web, so revenue understates them.
EstimatesAppfigures models downloads from ranks and ratings. Apps below its threshold show as "not modeled," which means small, not zero.
MethodKeyword Inspector searches in a signed-in session on Nov 11, top 15 results per keyword with 30-day US estimates for the top 10, plus the ratings report for every app named here, Nov 2024 to Nov 2026. No credits, no tracking slots. Raw data in ~/Projects/appfigures-ideas.

ReadRead these as revenue generated for the partner, not for the group; what the group gets is the equity or affiliate share it negotiates. That is the point: 1% of the audience on a $79-a-month panel is an $8M-a-year customer base, which is about 7% of Function's whole business and more than any single marketing channel a company that size has. That is the number to put in front of Rythm or Superpower.
How to read the bandsCreator-sold products convert 1% to 3% of an engaged audience, so the 1% row is the realistic case, 10% is a multi-year ceiling, and 50% is the size of the whole audience spending, which no product reaches. Category pools are third-party estimates whose definitions vary; the measured rows are Appfigures iOS net revenue and understate anything sold on the web.
