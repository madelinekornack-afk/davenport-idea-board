Buzz

Buzz is Block's open-source team chat for people and AI agents, built on Nostr: a relay, a desktop app and a CLI. Created on GitHub in March 2026, first release in May. The group runs its own relay with 63 agents across 181 channels and has written its own fixes for the gaps. The question on this tab: is anyone else using it, and are the chat giants losing anything to it?

ReadThe star count says developers noticed; the download curve says they are not staying. 468K downloads in June to 74K in October, with releases still shipping weekly. Buzz is not on any app store, so Appfigures has nothing on it; this is the whole public signal.
What the group addsEverything on the Buzz card: the mention bridge, the roster, channel staffing, the busy-agent rule. Those are the fixes a daily user needs and a 129-contributor project has not shipped.

New App Store ratings per month: the giants

A second usage proxy, independent of the download model. People rate apps they keep using.

SlackMicrosoft TeamsDiscordGoogle Chat
Slack is flat, not fallingAbout 200K US installs a month for two years, 440K to 630K worldwide, with September 2026 among the highest months in the series. No sign of mobile erosion.
Teams is flat with a spring spikeAround 1M US a month, spiking to 2.2M in May 2025. Nothing in 2026 reads as decline.
Discord: fewer installs, more moneyUS downloads are down about 40% from the July 2025 peak, while in-app revenue doubled to $8.3M a month. It is monetizing an installed base, not growing one.
What this says for BuzzNothing has moved. Buzz's own download curve is falling faster than any giant's. If share moves, it will be in a new category, agent-native team chat, where none of the three giants compete today, and where the group's daily use is the edge.
ScopeiPhone only. Android, desktop and web, where most Slack and Teams usage lives, are not here.

Nothing in chat is replacing DiscordEvery messenger is flat or down. WhatsApp, Signal and Telegram have the same rating velocity they had two years ago; Messenger has lost two thirds; Google Chat half; GroupMe and Google Meet are at zero. Discord itself is flat on ratings, which means its 40% download drop is mostly the install model and the gaming cycle, not people leaving.
What went up is talking to modelsChatGPT's new ratings went from 100K a month to 1M. Gemini 5x. Grok from zero to 100K. Claude from 2K to 36K at the March 2026 peak. Muse from Meta is the biggest launch in the sweep, 2.7M US installs in 30 days. Across the same two years that is the only category on the phone that grew by an order of magnitude.
The one human-chat exception is streamingKick went from 80 ratings a month to 5,600 when it started paying streamers in late 2025, and is now at 530K US installs a month to Twitch's 320K. That is share moving inside a category, which is what Buzz would have to do in team chat.
What this says for BuzzAttention did not move to a better chat app. It moved to a chat window with an agent on the other side. Buzz is the one product in this group that puts agents and people in the same channels, so the growth story is not "beat Slack," it is "be where the two kinds of chat meet." That is also the strongest argument on this board for building on it.
CaveatsRatings count people who rate, so a prompt change moves the line (Slack's jump in Aug 2025, Labcorp's in May 2026 on the Bloodwork tab). The AI apps' curves are so steep that no prompt change explains them. Messenger's decline could partly be Meta moving ratings prompts to Instagram.

ReadThe honest pool for Buzz is none of these: agent-native team chat is a category with no revenue yet. Slack is the comparable because it is the product a Buzz customer would stop paying for. One percent of Slack is a $30M company. The audience framing is weaker here than anywhere else on the board: 850K people who watch explainer videos are not 850K teams, so the realistic buyer count is a few thousand small teams and the people running agents at home.
How to read the bandsCreator-sold products convert 1% to 3% of an engaged audience, so the 1% row is the realistic case, 10% is a multi-year ceiling, and 50% is the size of the whole audience spending, which no product reaches. Category pools are third-party estimates whose definitions vary; the measured rows are Appfigures iOS net revenue and understate anything sold on the web.
