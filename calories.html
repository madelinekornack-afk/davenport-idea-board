The crossover already happenedCal AI has out-downloaded MyFitnessPal in the US every month since October 2025. In Oct 2026 it was 220K to 188K.
MFP still makes about 5x the moneyIt earns $31 per US download against Cal AI's $5. MFP's downloads fell 25% worldwide while revenue fell only 7%: it is squeezing more out of the base it has.
MacroFactor is the one that is actually growingSame downloads as last year (+4% US) and +50% revenue: $28M worldwide in twelve months on 1.37M installs. At $25 per US download it monetises better than MFP. It has out-earned Cal AI worldwide every month since June 2026, and in the US since August.
Cal AI's growth is slowingUS downloads were +43% a year ago on the Oct 26 pull. On the refreshed window they are +35%, and every month since January 2026 has been below the one before. The January spike was its best month ever and it has not held any of it.

What it isAn adaptive macro coach from Stronger By Science, the evidence-based lifting site run by Greg Nuckols and Eric Trexler. You log food and weigh in; it back-calculates your real energy expenditure and resets your targets weekly. Manual logging, no ads, no scolding. $71.99 a year or $11.99 a month; a Workouts app launched January 2026 and bundles at $89.99.
The MFP model, not the Cal AI model24K all-time US ratings against Cal AI's 375K, so a far smaller base, paying a real annual subscription. Revenue per US download is about $25 against Cal AI's $5. The June 2026 ratings spike (4,257) looks like an in-app prompt, not a surge of users.
What this says for idea 15MacroFactor is the one newcomer in the field that grew by being better rather than louder, and it is the only one whose revenue is still rising. A better Cal AI that charges like MacroFactor is the shape to aim at: Cal AI's volume with MacroFactor's retention.

Cal AI is not the front of a waveIts new ratings are down 73% from January 2026 (23K a month to 5.9K in October). Numify and BitePal each spiked past 5,000 a month in early 2026 and have faded by two thirds. Calo is the only AI-photo app still growing, from 53 a month to about 2,000.
The old guard is holdingMyNetDiary has doubled its ratings pace in two years, Cronometer and Noom are flat, Lose It! is up on a March 2026 spike. WeightWatchers and Lifesum are the ones fading. Nobody in the field is going away; the newcomers are not taking the incumbents' users, they are adding and then churning their own.
Who buys adsOn "calorie counter", MFP had 43% of Apple Ads impressions, Lose It 27%, Cal AI 15%, and Cal AI had been absent for about a month at the time of the pull. On "cal ai", a look-alike called Calor AI had taken 20% of the impressions on Cal AI's own name.
What this says for idea 16A newcomer can take the download lead in this category in a year, and the money is real: Cal AI cleared $28M on iOS in twelve months. But its growth has turned over, the photo-logging clones behind it are tiny, and MFP's revenue barely moved. The open ground is not another logger; it is the thing that sits on top of one, which is the health copilot pitch, and the reason the board scores its market 5 and its edge 2.

ReadCal AI took 28% of the measured two-app pool in two years from a standing start, so 10% is not a fantasy band in this category; it is roughly what a newcomer with a real hook and paid acquisition has done. The audience framing is strong: the people who watch Maxinomics are the people who track things, and 1% of them at $60 is a half-million-dollar business on its own.
How to read the bandsCreator-sold products convert 1% to 3% of an engaged audience, so the 1% row is the realistic case, 10% is a multi-year ceiling, and 50% is the size of the whole audience spending, which no product reaches.
