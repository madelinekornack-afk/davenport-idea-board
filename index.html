Clark: personal data pipeline for email, transactions, files

A personal system of record. Authenticate once and get vectorized email search, normalized transactions (classifier first, web search when unsure, email as evidence), and classified files, indexed together and open to any agent you choose. Phil's framing on Nov 11: every business queries a system of record all day; no individual has ever had one. A Mac and iOS app for email and transactions sits on top, because "it will not only be agents."

Why it ranks hereTwo of three of you already built and use it. Phil's version hits about 94% on merchant identification and filed his taxes (K-1, wash sales, a closed Fidelity rollover) in an hour and a half. Maddy's version found and submitted her FSA-eligible expenses to HealthEquity. Steve said Mercury put its best engineers on transaction categorization for six months.
EdgeIt exists. Phil on Nov 11: Clark is one of the three places the group has an edge, "because I've already built a lot of it." Steve's Books-on-Mercury experience covers the hardest part, and Max's audience is the launch channel. Steve flipped on CLI by the end: a trending "install this and your email is searchable" post would get him to try it.
Reddit signalThe loudest personal-finance ask found was a 259-upvote thread praising Monarch Money's receipt scanner and then listing what it cannot do: match Amazon and PayPal line items, edit splits, attach receipts that never link to the posted charge. That is the email-as-evidence step this pipeline already does. A separate thread calls medical bills "harder to track than anything else," with people keeping spreadsheets; five tiny bill-vs-EOB apps launched in 2026 and none has traction. Subscription trackers, by contrast, get the same reply every time: the bank app already does that.
RiskPhil's own words: "I'm not entirely sure it's useful for me... maybe it's just a bespoke internal tool." Steve: this is personal-AI-assistant territory (Instinct, Haven), which is crowded and where the big assistants are heading. Phil is hesitant to enter the agent world. Onboarding (email auth, Plaid consent) and privacy are the two named problems. Once someone connects everything, though, it is "wildly sticky." Packaging risk from Reddit: idea posts that open with "AI-powered" get dismissed on sight, and spending-tool users say repeatedly they will not pay a subscription, preferring free or one-time purchase.
Nov 11 debateSteve: "still not super clear to me how to package as a product, feels like infrastructure," and it drifts toward the personal AI assistant space (Muse, Instinct), which is "mega crowded, with lots of VC dollars." The non-technical use Muse; the technical already have their own setups and would "just ask Claude to go through Plaid." Phil's answer: he does not want an assistant on top, he wants "pure unfettered access to my data across all those data verticals, available to any system or agent I choose," for the cohort that will not lock their data into one provider. Steve, probing rather than arguing: that cohort is the hardest to sell structure to, and he would "just ask Claude to go through Plaid." Maddy's data point: she could not find Sarah and Dan's wedding website and wanted exactly the browser-history search.
Phil's answer (voice notes, Nov 11)"That's really useful to hear," and yes, Plaid alone is easy. The case that is not: he gets a revenue-share lump sum Friday, the statement is lighter than expected, and checking it means Slack, email, transactions and a spreadsheet, plus who signed which deal for how much and when a decision was later reversed. Hooking Claude Code to each source is bespoke, the question would run for an hour or two on a top model, and Slack does not work that way anyway. What it needs is a graph of events, people and decisions that validates and invalidates them over time, "running in the background all the time" so you can rely on it when you need it. "How to market exactly, still not entirely sure."
Steve's counter (three voice notes, 11:03 to 11:15)He already runs Claude over Slack, Notion, Linear, Gmail and GitHub at Mercury all day and it answers complex questions fast, so his two questions are: what is the technical missing piece versus an out-of-the-box agent setup, and what does a user actually gain? He can see one answer, background ingestion that tags things as they arrive ("this matters for getting paid"), but calls that hard to do better than the existing tools. Platform risk: funded startups keep building this shape and then Anthropic ships a feature that makes them obsolete. Whole thing feels "nebulous, across the board we're just making personal AI use better," which is "a really tall order." His proposal: shrink it to one problem area at a time, the best agentic setup for personal finance, or for relationships. Then, catching himself: "now we're getting into harness land," every vertical AI startup has an ingestion pipeline underneath, "we're still talking about infrastructure." Not against it, thinking it through.
Where Steve landed (11:33, "last one until I get feedback")Instinct apparently cracked the general personal assistant, and what makes it better than plain Claude is the infrastructure behind the app. Every vertical agentic app (CRM, SEO, whatever) does the same thing one level down, and every one of them puts a UI on top that locks you in. Phil's version is that backend without the lock-in. So the product might be "headless agentic apps: bring your own agent, we give you the backend," aimed at specific use cases rather than all of personal life. That is the first framing both of them have agreed on.
Where it standsThe Oct 22 call ended mid-sentence on packaging: CLI utility for tech-savvy users first, GUI route second, Plaid optional at signup. Maddy asked Phil on Oct 27 to onboard her; no sign that happened. Steve asked Phil for the repo on Nov 11; no answer yet. Phil is building the next layer now: email and transactions in one Mac app and one iOS app on top of the index, since people still want to see transactions, not just balances. Whiteboard question for tonight (Wed, Nov 11): Phil has described the thing (a personal system of record with a GUI on top) and Steve has named the gap (infrastructure, not yet a product). Steve's own fix is the agenda: pick one problem area. Personal finance is the obvious one, since the receipt-to-transaction matching and the revenue-share audit are both already there. Who pays for that first, what does the Anthropic-ships-it-next-quarter version look like, and is "headless, bring your own agent" the pitch or just the architecture?

Why it ranks hereIt sits on Orca, which Phil calls one of the two real edges, and neither he nor Steve has a packaging doubt about it the way they do about Clark. Market is a 3, not a 4: the 5M owner-to-PM relationships in the Fireworks analysis is funnel size, not buyers. Only owners who use a PM and suspect them are in play, and most of those switch PMs rather than buy software. Havyn charges $19 to 99/mo for the same promise, so some people pay. Steve's generalization, auditing any variable monthly vendor bill, is what keeps it at 3. Briefly rank 1 on Nov 11 before Maddy questioned the market score.
EdgeOrca is one of the three edges Phil named on Nov 11, and this is Orca pointed at owners. Steve sees the sloppy invoicing daily, and Orca's verified maintenance data is the moat nobody else has. Upload-and-check alone is a commodity.
RiskChannel conflict: an owner-side auditor is aimed at Orca's own PM customers. DoorVault gives the statement audit away free. Fireworks' read is that the recovery workflow is the product, the audit is only the hook.
Landlord forumsAcross three BiggerPockets threads on what landlords want from software, nobody asked to audit their PM's statement. What they asked for, in order: every tenant and vendor message in one auditable thread, maintenance tracked from report through vendor to payment, maintenance history that does not vanish so they stop "paying for the same problem twice," and hidden or creeping fees. That points at the PM-branded trust portal with a verified maintenance record, which Orca's data already is, rather than the owner-side auditor.
Where it standsSteve is considering an MVP as an owner-facing surface on Orca. Design concept exists, not iterated. Phil and Maddy have not weighed in yet.

And Phil's own instinct, said about his other tools, is "maybe it's just a bespoke internal tool."
Recommended wedgeSell the Performance tab alone first: connect YouTube, log packaging changes, see what moved by traffic source. A 30-second demo at $20 to 50 a month with every serious YouTuber as the funnel. The production suite is the upsell, and building it in public on Max is the marketing.
