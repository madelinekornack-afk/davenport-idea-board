What this says for Nora, idea 7

Open field, wrong shelfThere is no incumbent to displace, which supports the edge score. But the App Store is also telling you that the buyer Nora is for, a senior person who wants to learn by doing, does not go to the App Store for it. They buy a cohort course, a newsletter, a workshop, or they watch a video, which is where Maxinomics already is.
What that means for the buildNora as a Maxinomics product (a paid series, exercises delivered by email and in a browser, a Buzz channel to do them in) fits the data. Nora as an iOS app with in-app purchase would be the seventy-fifth entry in a category where the seventy-fourth has six ratings.
Market scoreStays at 3. The market is real and the audience is in it, but it is off-store, so none of it shows up in the data here. Pricing comps are Maven cohorts ($500 to $2,000) and Section's AI courses, not $9.99 a month.
ScopeUS, iPhone, App Store only. Android, web sign-ups and web billing are not here, and most of these companies sell on the web, so revenue understates them.
EstimatesAppfigures models downloads from ranks and ratings. Apps below its threshold show as "not modeled," which means small, not zero.

ReadThere is no App Store pool to take a share of, which is why the audience framing is the whole model here. At creator-course conversion rates (1% to 3% of an engaged audience buy), Nora is a $2.5M to $7.5M product if it is sold to the Maxinomics audience directly, and the 10% band is the ceiling a well-run cohort business reaches over several years, not a launch number.
How to read the bandsCreator-sold products convert 1% to 3% of an engaged audience, so the 1% row is the realistic case, 10% is a multi-year ceiling, and 50% is the size of the whole audience spending, which no product reaches. Category pools are third-party estimates whose definitions vary; the measured rows are Appfigures iOS net revenue and understate anything sold on the web.
